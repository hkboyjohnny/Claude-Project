COFCO Maintains Soy Moratorium Despite Pushback, Anticipates a Rise in Brazil Grain Shipment, and Invests in New Santos Port Terminal

Chinese state-owned grain trader COFCO has announced that it will continue to observe a cessation on soybean purchases from Brazil, despite the outcry from local farmers who request for relaxation of the policy. Allan Virtanen, COFCO's Global Head of Communications and Sustainability, stated this at a news conference, declining to give further commentary. The 'soy moratorium' is an optional initiative undertaken by global grain traders to resist acquisition of soybeans cultivated in the Amazonian regions deforested after 2008.

Soy Moratorium and COFCO

COFCO's Past and Predicted Grain Shipment

In 2024, COFCO, recognized as one of Brazil's largest grain exporters, transported 17 million tonnes of soybeans and corn from the country. This volume is projected to slightly increase in 2025 to 18 million tonnes as disclosed by Sergio Ferreira, COFCO's Chief Operating Officer in Brazil during the same news conference. Ferreira's estimates however, do not account for the potential impact of a brewing trade conflict between the U.S. and China which could, if it materializes, give Brazilian soy a competitive advantage.

About 80 percent of the grain COFCO exports from Brazil is directed towards China, Ferreira disclosed. However, the officials were unable to ascertain the proportion of their grain shipments that come from the U.S., one of Brazil's chief competitors in global grain trade alongside Argentina. What's clear though, is that the majority of grains exported by COFCO are sourced from Brazil.

COFCO's Expansion at the Port of Santos

Meanwhile, the company is making significant strides in infrastructure, set to commence operations at a new grain terminal at the port of Santos in Sao Paulo as soon as April. This terminal, christened STS-11, will manage 14.5 million tonnes of commodities each year when fully operational in 2026. The commodities include soybeans, corn, sugar, and soymeal.

This new venture has received an investment of $285 million for its establishment. Given Brazil's crucial role in COFCO's business, STS-11 will be the company's largest export port terminal globally, reaffirming the country's importance to the grain trader.

Soybean Argentina Weather Report 11th October 2026

Argentina&#x27;s soybean regions are sitting in a cool, wet pre-planting window. Rainfall over the past two weeks has been well above average across the core Pampa, and soils are entering the planting season in good shape. Temperatures have been running below seasonal norms, with daytime highs notably subdued, but nights have stayed mild and there is no frost threat in the data.

Soybean Austria Weather Report 11th October 2026

Austrian soybeans are at or past harvest across all growing regions. The dry, warm autumn conditions that have persisted through early October are typical of the Pannonian climate at this time of year and have no bearing on 2026 production, which was determined months ago during flowering and pod fill.
