# Sinochem Holdings: sector peer comparison (Energy, Agriculture, Rubber)

*Prepared 12 October 2026. Basis: fiscal year to 31 December 2025 (filed or audited statements), with H1 2026 read-through where companies have reported. Companion workbook: `Sinochem_Peer_Comparison.xlsx` (every ratio is a live formula; inputs on the `Data` sheet).*

## 1. Bottom line

Sinochem Holdings (中国中化控股) is unlisted and publishes no sector P&L, so I anchored each sector on the group entities that do publish full statements and compared them with 5 to 8 listed peers per sector on the same ratio definitions.

| Sector | Sinochem anchor | Position vs listed peers | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Energy** | Sinochem Energy (中化能源) | **Weakest of the three** | Bottom third on 5 of 9 scorecard metrics. EBITDA margin 1.3% vs 8.5% peer median, EBITDA/assets 4.8% vs 8.1%, EBIT does not cover interest (−0.4x). Net loss narrowed to RMB 1.7bn (FY24: RMB 3.2bn), and cash flow was helped by working-capital release. |
| **Agriculture** | Syngenta AG, with ADAMA, Yangnong, Sinofert | **Mixed: strong franchise, diluted reported earnings, heavy balance sheet** | Syngenta AG is bottom third on 7 of 9 metrics as reported (USD 792m litigation provisions, USD 225m restructuring, USD 774m finance cost, tax above pre-tax profit). Adding back the first two puts its EBITDA margin near 18%, above the 16.8% median. Yangnong is top third on 7 of 9; ADAMA is bottom third on 7 of 9 but turning. |
| **Rubber** | Pirelli, with Aeolus Tyre | **Strongest of the three** | Pirelli's EBITDA margin of 21.6% is the best in the peer set (median 15.5%) and its FCF margin of 13.7% is the highest. But Pirelli's own accounts state that no entity controls it (Sinochem holds about 34%), so Sinochem's exposure is a large minority stake, not consolidated results. Aeolus is a lower-margin, sub-scale follower. |

Three things cut across the sectors:

- **Leverage is the common weak spot.** Sinochem Energy runs 4.5x net debt/EBITDA (peer median 2.2x); Syngenta AG 4.7x and ADAMA 4.1x (CP and seeds median 1.3x). That fits the group picture: the main financing platform, Sinochem Corp (中国中化股份), reports a 68% capitalisation ratio and total debt/EBITDA of 15.3x for FY2025.
- **FY2025 predates the 2026 refining upturn, and Sinochem's share of it is unclear.** In H1 2026 Sinopec's net profit rose 19%, PetroChina's 22%, and Hengli's more than doubled; Sinochem Energy has no H1 2026 statement I could find, and it cut refinery run rates in Q1 2026.
- **Reported profit overstates operating strength at group level.** For Sinochem Corp in FY2025, investment income plus fair-value gains were RMB 11.6bn against RMB 1.4bn of operating business profit.

**Performance of Sinochem Energy in FY2025.**

- Revenue fell 7.7% to RMB 394.1bn on lower crude prices. The net loss narrowed to RMB 1.68bn from RMB 3.18bn.
- Quality of earnings is thin. The operating loss of RMB 0.97bn includes a RMB 1.0bn fair-value gain and RMB 0.65bn of asset impairments. Tax expense of RMB 0.74bn was booked on a pre-tax *loss* of RMB 0.94bn. EBITDA of RMB 5.0bn (+57%) falls to about RMB 4.0bn without the fair-value gain.
- The trend in the segment is down: petrochemical gross margin at Sinochem Corp went 2.90% → 2.17% → 1.81% over FY2023–25.
- Cash flow looks strong (operating cash flow RMB 6.7bn, 134% of EBITDA) but RMB 2.5bn of that is working-capital release, and capex was cut to RMB 1.5bn from RMB 2.4bn. Net debt is RMB 22.3bn (4.5x EBITDA), interest expense of RMB 1.09bn exceeds EBIT, and perpetual bonds in equity rose to RMB 7.05bn from RMB 5.05bn.

**Peer comparison.**

1. **The margin gap is mostly business mix, so read returns on assets, not margins.** Trading and wholesale/retail are about 87% of pre-elimination segment revenue; refining is 12%. Gross margin (1.6%) is therefore structurally thin. On EBITDA/average assets, Sinochem Energy earns 4.8% vs 8.1% for the peer median (state majors 12.7%, private refiners 8.3%). Closing that 3.3-point gap on average assets of about RMB 104bn would take roughly RMB 3.4bn more EBITDA a year.
2. **Taxes need adjusting before comparing with Chinese refiners.** They book consumption tax below gross profit (Sinopec: RMB 252bn, 9.1% of revenue; Sinochem Energy: 0.9%). After taxes and surcharges, the margin is 0.7% at Sinochem Energy vs 6.8% Sinopec, 13.1% PetroChina, 8.7% Hengli, 4.4% Rongsheng and 0.6% Sinopec Shanghai. Only Sinopec Shanghai looks similar.
3. **Leverage is above the median but not the worst.** 4.5x vs 2.2x median; Hengli (4.7x) and Rongsheng (7.4x) are heavier after their mega-projects. The difference is coverage: both of those earn positive EBIT cover (3.4x, 1.5x); Sinochem Energy does not (−0.4x).
4. **Cash conversion ranks top third, but it is flattered** by the working-capital release noted above.
5. **Net position:** Sinochem Energy sits with Sinopec Shanghai at the bottom of the group; the leaders (PetroChina, Hengli) combine upstream integration or very large integrated refining-chemicals scale.

**What changes the 2026 picture.** Peers' H1 2026 profits jumped with crude prices (Sinopec parent net profit +19%, PetroChina +22%, Hengli RMB 7.2bn vs RMB 3.1bn, Rongsheng RMB 9.3bn vs RMB 1.7bn, both net income before minorities; Sinopec Shanghai back in profit). At Sinochem Corp, Q1 2026 total profit rose to RMB 4.2bn from RMB 1.1bn, but RMB 2.8bn of that was fair-value gains (mostly the Salt Lake stake); operating business profit was RMB 0.54bn vs RMB 0.52bn. The rating agency also notes the company deliberately cut refinery run rates in Q1 2026. My inference is that Sinochem Energy will participate less in the refining upcycle than throughput-heavy peers; its H1 2026 statements will test this.

**Performance of the Sinochem agriculture entities.**

- **Syngenta Group (headline):** FY2025 sales USD 28.4bn (−1%, +2% excluding the planned cut in grain trading), EBITDA USD 4.4bn (+13%), margin 15.4% (+1.9 points). H1 2026: sales USD 12.2bn (−2%), EBITDA USD 2.4bn, margin 19.5% vs 18.6%. Sinofert was transferred to Sinochem Hong Kong on 23 December 2025 and left the Group's consolidation from January 2026.
- **Syngenta AG:** gross margin 39.5% (CP and seeds median 28.0%; Corteva 47.3%), so the franchise is intact. Below the line: other operating expense of USD 1.14bn includes USD 792m of litigation provisions, USD 225m of restructuring, finance expense USD 774m, and tax of USD 765m on pre-tax profit of USD 613m, giving a net loss of USD 152m. Reported EBITDA margin is 12.4% vs a 16.8% median; adding back litigation and restructuring gives about 18.4% (illustrative; peers are not adjusted).
- **ADAMA:** EBITDA up 96% to RMB 3.4bn and gross margin up 3.4 points to 26.3%, but a net loss of RMB 1.0bn (FY24: RMB 2.9bn) on finance costs and impairments, with interest cover of 1.3x. H1 2026 net profit was RMB 433m vs a RMB 80m loss, so the turnaround is real.
- **Yangnong:** the standout: EBITDA margin 25.8%, ROE 11.7%, net cash, revenue +13.8%.
- **Sinofert:** revenue RMB 23.3bn (+9.4%), ROE 11.5%, net cash. EBITDA margin of 8.5% vs 21.8% for crop-nutrition peers reflects a distribution-led model rather than weak operations.

**Peer comparison.**

1. **The gap to peers is the balance sheet and one-offs, not the operating franchise.** Net debt of USD 9.9bn is 4.7x reported EBITDA (3.2x excluding the two items) vs a 1.3x median; Corteva is net cash. About USD 4.1bn of Syngenta AG's borrowings are from fellow Syngenta Group companies, so external leverage is lower than the headline. Ratings are investment grade (BBB / BBB / Baa3).
2. **Cash conversion is in line** (72% of EBITDA vs 72% median) but FCF margin is only 1.8% because capex includes USD 690m of capitalised development.
3. **Chinese crop-protection peers are growing faster at smaller scale** (Jiangshan +7%, Lier +23% revenue, EBITDA margins 16–20%); Yangnong is the group's answer to them and ranks top of the set.
4. **FMC shows the downside case** (USD 2.2bn net loss, negative EBITDA, revenue −18%); it is why the CP/seeds medians are best read alongside Corteva.
5. **Scale:** Syngenta Group's sales of USD 28.4bn exceed Corteva (USD 17.4bn) and Nutrien (USD 26.9bn), but its margins and returns on reported numbers do not.

- **Aeolus Tyre:** revenue RMB 7.4bn (+10.5%), EBITDA margin 8.1%, net margin 3.2%, ROE 6.9%, leverage 0.4x. H1 2026 revenue +8%, net profit RMB 106m (flat).

**Peer comparison.**

1. **Pirelli leads on profitability and cash**: EBITDA margin 21.6% vs Michelin 16.6%, Bridgestone 16.6%, and a 15.5% peer median; net margin 7.8% vs 6.4%; ROE of 8.6% is in line (8.9%).
2. **Growth is the gap:** Pirelli's revenue was flat vs Chinese producers at +12–16%, but those peers run negative or near-zero free cash flow (Sailun −1.7%, Zhongce −3.6%) and Linglong's H1 2026 net profit fell to RMB 101m from RMB 854m.
3. **Aeolus trails its Chinese peers on margin** (8.1% vs 12.5–18.1%), at roughly one-sixth of Zhongce's revenue, with the lowest leverage in the group as the offset.
4. **Goodyear is the cautionary peer** (net loss USD 1.7bn, 6.5x leverage).
5. **Control is the main caveat for Pirelli.** Following Italy's 2023 golden-power decree, Pirelli's board approved accounts in April 2025 (9 of 15 directors in favour) stating that Sinochem, through Marco Polo International Italy, no longer controls Pirelli under IFRS 10 and that no entity does; directors representing Sinochem voted against. After the December 2025 bond conversion Sinochem holds about 34.1% (from about 37%), MTP S.p.A. (Camfin and affiliates) about 25.3%, and the free float about 40.6%. Reuters reported in October 2025 that Sinochem was weighing a sale, and three directors (Zhang Haitao, Xi Xiaohong, Wang Kun) voted against Pirelli's H1 2026 report over its declaration of control by MTP S.p.A. So treat Pirelli as a valuable, non-controlled holding: Sinochem's exposure is its 34% stake, not the consolidated EUR 6.8bn of revenue.
6. **Natural rubber is no longer in the picture.** Sinochem International's natural-rubber segment went from RMB 18.1bn of revenue (2022) to RMB 1.1bn (2023) and nil (2024–25). It agreed in November 2022 to sell 36% of Halcyon Agri to Hainan Rubber; I did not verify completion or Sinochem's remaining stake. Hainan Rubber's profile (EBITDA margin 2.3%, 12.2x leverage) shows how thin natural-rubber economics are, which fits Sinochem International's stated 2022 aim of refocusing on materials science.

## 8. Method, data and limits

- **Same definitions for every company.** EBITDA = EBIT + D&A with no add-back for impairments, restructuring or litigation (so it differs from company "adjusted EBITDA"). Debt includes leases (US GAAP filers exclude operating leases). ROE/ROA use average equity/assets. Full definitions are in the workbook README.
- **Entity-level, not group-level.** Sinochem Energy and Syngenta AG are unlisted and compared on their filed statements; Syngenta Group's consolidated net income and balance sheet are not published.
- **Sources.** Sinochem Energy FY2025 audited statements (read from the scanned PDF and reconciled: assets = liabilities + equity); Sinochem Corp rating reports; Syngenta, Pirelli, Michelin, Bridgestone and Nutrien filings; SEC XBRL for Corteva, FMC, Mosaic and Goodyear; Eastmoney F10 for China A-share and Sinofert statements. I spot-checked Eastmoney against independent sources (Sinofert and Sinochem International FY2025 results, ADAMA's revenue in Syngenta Group's release: all tie). Excel ratios were matched to an independent Python calculation (540 comparisons, differences below 0.01%).
- **Not covered.** No valuation multiples (Sinochem is unlisted and market data was not pulled). The S&P Capital IQ connector in this session needs authorisation; once authorised I can cross-check peer figures and add consensus and valuation.
- **Currency.** USD sizes use 2025 average FX for both P&L and balance sheet (indicative); ratios are currency-neutral.
