# Briefing Analysis — Cross-Border Direct Lending from Shanghai Branch to Leoni AG

**Status:** Internal draft for discussion. Not legal advice. Regulatory references must be confirmed by Shanghai Branch Compliance / Legal before any commitment.
**Prepared:** 9 Nov 2026 · **Public evidence:** see `03_Public_Disclosures.md` (sources A1–A8 referenced below)

## 1. Purpose

Assess whether Shanghai Branch can extend a **cross-border direct loan (境外贷款 / 跨境直贷)** to **Leoni AG** (Nuremberg, Germany), now controlled by **Luxshare Precision Industry Co., Ltd. (立讯精密)**, and identify the points Compliance must clear. The decisive issue is **use of proceeds**: the loan would refinance a **Luxshare shareholder loan**, which itself was used to repay Leoni's **2023 syndicated loan**.

## 3. Use-of-proceeds chain (look-through test)

```
New loan (Shanghai Branch)
   └─► repays Luxshare shareholder loan          ← Q1: was the SHL used to repay debt?  YES
          └─► SHL repaid 2023 syndicated loan     ← Q2: was the SHL used for dividends?  NO
                 └─► Syndicated loan financed Leoni working capital  (evidence pending)
```

Compliance will look through each layer. The answer at every layer must be **real-economy, operating purpose**, with no dividend or capital extraction and no disguised round-tripping to the PRC.

## 4. The two key questions

### Q1 — Was the shareholder loan used to repay Leoni's debt? → **Affirmative**

- **Basis:** (i) public disclosure (see below); (ii) confirmation from counsel.
- **Public evidence:**
  - Luxshare announcement **2024-075** says Luxshare HK and its affiliates will provide related-party loans 「用于提前偿还因本交易触发的 Leoni 集团的任何相关财务债务（包括但不限于现有的循环信贷额度、本票债券、保理和反向保理、其他本地贷款）」 (A1). In English: to prepay any Leoni group financial debt triggered by the transaction, including the existing revolving credit facilities, promissory notes, factoring and reverse factoring, and other local loans.
  - Clifford Chance: "a substantial part of LEONI's financial liabilities were refinanced through Luxshare" at the 10 Jul 2025 closing (A3).
  - Dentons: the restructured note liabilities were fully repaid (A4).
- **Why it matters:** It shows the shareholder loan replaced existing third-party bank debt. Our loan therefore refinances **operating debt**. It does not fund new capital extraction.
- **Evidence to file:**
  - Luxshare announcement(s) on the acquisition and the shareholder loan (SZSE filings), and Leoni disclosures.
  - Shareholder loan agreement (amount, date, purpose clause).
  - Syndicated loan repayment / payoff letter and release confirmations from the facility agent.
  - Bank statements tracing SHL drawdown → syndicate repayment (amounts and dates match).
  - Legal opinion / counsel confirmation letter (German counsel, plus PRC counsel if available).

### Q2 — Was the shareholder loan used for dividends? → **Negative (per client)**

- **Basis:** Client feedback, supported by public evidence but **not yet sufficient on its own**:
  - No dividend by Leoni is disclosed anywhere.
  - The SPA prohibited 「减资或利润分配」 (capital reductions or profit distributions) before closing (A1).
  - Leoni AG lost EUR 128m in FY2023 (A1), and its share capital was cut to zero under the 2023 StaRUG plan (A6). It therefore had no distributable profit available.
- **Why it matters:** Dividends would mean capital went to shareholders, not to operations. The most sensitive case is a payment upstream to the PRC parent, which could be viewed as onshore funds disguised as an overseas loan and flowing back to China (变相回流).
- **Evidence to obtain:**
  - Client written representation that no SHL proceeds were used for dividends, capital reductions or other shareholder distributions.
  - Leoni audited financial statements / cash flow statement for the relevant period(s) showing no dividend paid (Leoni had just come out of its 2023 StaRUG restructuring, so a distribution would be unusual and easy to check).
  - Representation and covenant in our facility agreement: no distributions funded by the loan.

### Supporting point — the 2023 syndicated loan financed working capital *(proof to be provided)*

- Request: 2023 syndicated facility agreement (purpose clause), utilisation requests, and management/CFO certificate on use.
- Together with Q1 and Q2, this establishes that the economic purpose is **working capital refinancing**.

## 5. Cross-border regulatory considerations (to be confirmed by Compliance)

1. **Regulatory route and eligibility**
   - *RMB loan:* PBOC/SAFE framework for overseas lending by domestic banks (银发〔2022〕27号, effective 1 Jan 2023). It covers eligible borrowers (offshore enterprises invested by domestic enterprises), the macro-prudential overseas lending cap (境外贷款余额上限), and RCPMIS reporting.
   - *FX loan (e.g., EUR):* The bank's own FX overseas lending falls under the same macro-prudential cap and external claims (对外债权) reporting.
   - *If Luxshare guarantees:* This becomes **内保外贷** under SAFE 汇发〔2014〕29号, with its own registration and use-of-funds rules (see point 3).
   - **Ask:** Which route applies, and is there headroom under the Branch / Head Office cap?

2. **Use of proceeds must stay offshore / real-economy**
   - The overseas lending rules require the funds to serve the borrower's genuine overseas business and prohibit direct or indirect repatriation to the PRC.
   - **Core issue:** Does repaying the shareholder loan amount to repatriation to the PRC?
  - **Public disclosure helps here:** the shareholder-loan lender is **香港立讯 (Hong Kong) or its affiliates**, not the PRC listed parent (A1). On that basis, the repayment stays **offshore** (Germany → Hong Kong) and does not flow directly back into China.
  - **Remaining questions for Compliance:**
    - (a) Confirm that the actual lender entity is offshore.
    - (b) If 香港立讯 funded the loan from onshore money (for example, onshore Luxshare's 境外放款 or ODI funds), is repayment to Hong Kong still acceptable, or would it be viewed as indirect repatriation (变相回流)?
    - (c) Should the loan agreement restrict the onward use of repaid funds?
   - **Mitigants to present:**
     - The SHL was originally outbound capital properly registered by Luxshare.
     - The SHL was used offshore to repay third-party bank debt (Q1).
     - No dividends were paid (Q2).
     - The underlying purpose was working capital.
     - Repaying the SHL restores Luxshare's registered outbound lending position. It is not new money entering the PRC for unrelated use.
   - **Fallback structures if (b):** Pay into a Luxshare offshore entity (not onshore); refinance only the portion not traceable to the PRC parent; or restrict the use of proceeds to new working capital or third-party debt.

3. **Guarantee structure (if any)**
   - 内保外贷 rules restrict guaranteed proceeds from being returned to the PRC through lending, equity or securities investment without SAFE approval. Repaying a PRC parent's loan needs a specific view.
   - **Ask:** Is a standalone direct loan to Leoni, with a keepwell or comfort letter instead of a guarantee, preferable?

4. **Luxshare's ODI and SHL registrations**
   - Confirm NDRC / MOFCOM ODI filings and SAFE registration for the Leoni acquisition.
   - If the SHL was funded from onshore, confirm Luxshare's overseas lending registration (境外放款登记) for it.
   - Gaps here would undermine the "lawful repayment channel" argument.

5. **KYC / sanctions / industry policy**
   - Leoni (automotive wiring and cables) fits encouraged ODI sectors.
   - Standard EU/US sanctions screening, beneficial ownership checks and AML apply.

6. **German law points (for Legal)**
   - Shareholder loans are subordinated in insolvency (§39(1) No. 5 InsO), and repayments within one year before an insolvency filing can be challenged (§135 InsO).
   - Our loan's repayment of the SHL is therefore a point to cover in the credit analysis and legal opinion, in light of Leoni's 2023 restructuring history.
   - Also consider capital maintenance (§57 AktG) where any upstream security or guarantee is given.

**Bottom line:** The use-of-proceeds chain (debt refinancing → working capital, no dividends) supports feasibility. The **gating item** is Compliance's view on whether repaying a PRC-parent shareholder loan is acceptable under the overseas lending and round-tripping rules. Raise this with Shanghai Compliance first (see `02_Email_to_Shanghai_Compliance.md`), before term sheet discussions.
